# Task 4.3 — Roles

## Objective

Give the four roles meaning: what each may do, who may change whose, and the
rules that stop an organisation becoming unadministrable.

## Context

4.2 stored a role and used it for nothing. The `testing` skill is explicit that a
permission system with no negative tests is decoration, so the tests here are
mostly about what each role *cannot* do.

## Implementation steps

1. A permission enum and a role→permissions table, defined once.
2. `TenantScope.can(permission)` and a `require` that raises.
3. Role-change rules: nobody may grant above their own level, nobody may change
   their own role, only an owner may create another owner.
4. The last owner cannot be demoted or removed.
5. Tests for every refusal.

**Excluded:** endpoints (4.5, 4.6), invitations (4.4), resource-level checks (4.6).

## Acceptance criteria

- Each role's permission set is asserted explicitly, including what it lacks.
- An admin cannot promote anyone to owner.
- Nobody can change their own role, including an owner.
- A member or viewer cannot change anyone's role.
- The last owner cannot be demoted or removed; a second owner makes it possible.
- Removing a member is refused for anyone below admin.
- `scripts/check.sh` passes.

## Tests

Unit for the permission table; integration for the change rules.

### Deviations
- **Permission sets are written out per role, not derived from the rank.**
  Deriving them makes it impossible to give a role a capability another above it
  lacks, and hides which role gained a permission when the table changes. The
  ladder is then asserted by a test rather than assumed by construction.
- **`require` raises 403, unlike a non-member's 404.** The caller has already
  proved they belong here, so naming the missing permission tells them nothing
  they did not know and tells them what to ask an administrator for.
- **Nobody may change their own role, including an owner.** The alternative is
  an owner demoting themselves and leaving nobody able to administer the
  organisation — recoverable only by a database edit.
- **The last-owner guard covers removal as well as demotion.** Removing the last
  owner strands the organisation exactly as demoting them would; guarding only
  one is guarding neither.

### Known limitations
- The permission table names capabilities for phases 5, 7 and 10 that nothing
  checks yet. Naming them now keeps the table one readable list rather than
  something that grows by accident; the risk is a name that turns out wrong.
- No per-resource permissions — a member can edit any document in the
  organisation. Resource-level authorisation is 4.6.
- No audit trail of role changes. They are logged, but a log is not a record
  anyone can query; that belongs with 10.1.
